The Veronese table

Verona’s cooking is hearty, wine-soaked and proudly unfashionable. November is the right season for most of it.

Lesso con pearà

Boiled meats with pearà: a thick sauce of breadcrumbs, beef marrow, broth and a great deal of black pepper. A cold-season dish, served roughly October to March.

Pastissada de caval

Horse meat marinated in Valpolicella wine with spices, stewed for hours and served with polenta. Legend ties it to 489, when Theodoric beat Odoacer outside Verona and the fallen horses were cooked in wine. Historians are sceptical, but the city has made it an official local dish anyway.

Bigoli con l’arna

Thick, rough pasta squeezed through a bronze press, with duck ragù. The name comes from the dialect bigat, caterpillar.

Risotto all’Amarone

Risotto finished with Amarone, the big red made from grapes dried for months in the Valpolicella hills.

Gnocchi

Verona’s carnival is the Venerdì Gnocolar, ruled by the Papà del Gnoco. The San Zeno district’s Bacanal del Gnoco is said to commemorate a food handout during the 1531 famine.

Pandoro and nadalin

Pastry chef Domenico Melegatti patented the pandoro in Verona on 14 October 1894, with an eight-pointed star mould designed by the painter Angelo Dall’Oca Bianca. Its flatter ancestor, the nadalin, goes back much further. Mid-November is when both start appearing in pastry shops.

In the glass

Valpolicella, Amarone and sweet Recioto from the hills to the north-west; Soave white from the east; Bardolino from Lake Garda. Monte Veronese cheese from the Lessinia uplands goes with all of them. A glass of wine is an ombra.

Where to eat along the route

  • Caffè Borsari, Corso Porta Borsari. Tiny historic coffee bar, right on the Roman decumanus.
  • Osteria al Duca, Via Arche Scaligere. Lunch inside the medieval house of the Montecchi, Romeo’s family.
  • Osteria Sottoriva, Via Sottoriva. Under medieval porticoes by the river: the end-of-day ombra.
  • Antica Bottega del Vino, off Via Mazzini. A wine institution since 1890.
  • Trattoria al Bersagliere, Filippini area. Family trattoria since 1928, a reference for pastissada.

Before you go

Opening hours shift with the season. Check Torre dei Lamberti, the Arena, Castelvecchio, Juliet’s House, the Teatro Romano and the Scavi Scaligeri on the day.

If you plan to enter several of these, a VeronaCard can work out cheaper than separate tickets. Check what it currently includes.

Sant’Anastasia charges a small entry fee for visitors.

The day is timed for a mid-November sunset, around 16:45. In summer, push the afternoon later.
